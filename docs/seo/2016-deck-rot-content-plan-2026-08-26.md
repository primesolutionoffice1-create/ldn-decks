## Market Problem

Decks built around 2016 are now reaching a practical inspection milestone in Northern Virginia. They are not automatically old, but they have already been exposed to ten humid summers, repeated freeze-thaw cycles, shaded-lot moisture, pollen, leaf buildup, and storm water.

The strongest homeowner pain point is hidden framing risk: the surface boards may look acceptable while the pressure-treated frame underneath has joist-top rot, ledger water damage, post base decay, stair stringer rot, footing movement, or corroded hardware.

Priority markets:
- Fairfax County and Fairfax City: shaded mature lots, older infill homes, hidden ledger and stair problems.
- Loudoun County: Ashburn, Brambleton, Broadlands, Leesburg, Sterling, Aldie, South Riding; many HOA-governed 2010s decks and composite upgrades.
- Prince William County: Gainesville, Haymarket, Bristow, Manassas, Woodbridge, Dale City; elevated decks, slopes, clay soil, drainage and footing stress.

## Lead Conversion Notes

Best offer:
Free 10-year deck inspection with repair/resurface/replace recommendation.

Best form language:
- "Was your deck built around 2016?"
- "Are you seeing soft boards, loose railings, dark framing, or stair movement?"
- "Upload photos of joists, ledger, post bases, stairs, and surface boards."

Best sales positioning:
The homeowner does not need to know whether the project is a small repair or a full rebuild. LDN Decks should own the diagnosis and give a written scope.

## Avoid

- Do not claim every 2016 deck is unsafe.
- Do not guarantee ranking, lead volume, or exact repair cost without inspection.
- Do not create fear-only messaging. Use practical inspection-first language.
- Do not say Trex/composite causes rot. The frame rots from moisture details, drainage, flashing, ventilation, and installation conditions.
