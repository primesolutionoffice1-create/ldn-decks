# Content: Hidden Content

When building websites, you'll often encounter requests to hide content behind accordions, mouseovers, or collapsible blocks. While these design elements might seem appealing from an aesthetic perspective, they can significantly (and negatively) impact your SEO performance.

![[images/image27.png]]

What Is Hidden Content?

Hidden content refers to any text, images, or other elements that require user interaction to become visible. Common examples include:

* Accordion menus (expandable/collapsible sections)  
* Tab interfaces  
* Mouseover reveals  
* "Read more" expandable blocks  
* Content that appears on click

How Google Crawls Hidden Content

Google renders websites using the most recent version of Chrome. Here's the crucial detail: **Google's crawler can follow links, but it cannot click or hover**.

This means if your content requires any user interaction to become visible, Google's ability to properly index and rank that content is compromised.

The Technical Reality

While Google *can* technically see hidden content (you can verify this by searching for exact-match phrases in quotes), it doesn't treat hidden content equally to visible content.

💡 **Real-World Test**: In controlled experiments where identical websites were compared—some with hidden content and others with fully visible content—the sites with visible content consistently ranked higher when searching for the exact phrases contained within.

Best Practices for Content Visibility

The most straightforward approach is the most effective: **don't hide content**. Display all your important information directly on the page without requiring user interaction. You'll get:

1. **Better Rankings**: Visible content ranks better and faster than hidden content  
2. **User Expectations**: Users are accustomed to seeing content displayed openly on well-ranking websites  
3. **Crawl Efficiency**: Google can process your content more effectively when it's immediately accessible

📝 **Related Lesson**: This principle ties directly into our lesson on [[key-google-rank-factors|Google Rank Factors]], where content accessibility is a key ranking signal.
