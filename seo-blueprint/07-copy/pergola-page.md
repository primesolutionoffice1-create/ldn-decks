## H2 — "Pergola vs Pavilion vs Gazebo — Which Fits Your Backyard?"

> The three structures get used interchangeably but they're different builds:
>
> - **Pergola** — open or partially shaded roof of beams/louvers; not waterproof; modern, minimal.
> - **Pavilion** — fully roofed structure (shingles or metal); waterproof; traditional silhouette; larger footprint.
> - **Gazebo** — typically octagonal or hexagonal, fully roofed, often elevated, decorative.
>
> Pergolas suit modern and transitional homes. Pavilions suit larger lots and traditional homes. Gazebos suit garden-style use cases.
>

## H2 — "Permits and HOAs for NoVA Pergolas"

> Permit requirements vary by jurisdiction and structure size:
>
> - **Loudoun County** — pergolas under 200 sq ft attached to a residence may not require a permit (verify per project); over 200 sq ft, yes.
> - **Fairfax County** — most pergolas require a permit; freestanding under 256 sq ft may be exempt (case-specific).
> - **Prince William** — typically requires a permit if attached to the home or for any electrical integration.
> - **Arlington** — most permanent structures require a permit.
>
> When the pergola integrates with a deck or includes electrical, lighting, or motorized features, a permit is almost always required. We handle the permit when needed.
>
> Most NoVA HOAs require Architectural Review Committee approval. We've been approved through every major Loudoun, Fairfax, and Prince William HOA. [HOA rules across NoVA](/hoa-deck-rules-northern-virginia).

## H2 — "Pergola FAQs"

**Q: How much does a pergola cost in Northern Virginia?**
A: A simple 12×16 cedar pergola runs $7,000–$12,000 installed. A 14×20 motorized louvered pergola (premium tier) runs $25,000–$45,000. The biggest cost drivers are size, material, and whether it's a basic shade structure or a fully-integrated outdoor room with lighting, fans, screens, and heaters.

**Q: Do I need a permit for a pergola in NoVA?**
A: It depends on size, attachment, and electrical integration. Small freestanding pergolas may be exempt; attached pergolas, larger structures, and any with electric typically require permits. We handle the permit when required.

**Q: How long does a pergola last?**
A: Cedar 15–25 years with periodic re-staining. Vinyl 25–40 years (cosmetic discoloration possible). Aluminum 30+ years. Louvered systems carry 10–25 year mechanical warranties.

**Q: Can a pergola be added to an existing deck?**
A: Yes, when the existing deck and footings can support the additional structural load. We perform a structural review at consultation. Many NoVA decks were built without anticipating future pergola load and need supplemental support.

**Q: What's a louvered pergola?**
A: Adjustable aluminum louvers that rotate to open for sun or close for rain protection. Motorized via remote or app. Brands include StruXure, Renson, Pergola Kits Direct (PKD). Best when you want year-round outdoor use.

**Q: Do you build pavilions and gazebos too?**
A: Yes — pavilions (fully roofed) and gazebos. Different structural calc and permit path; different price range. We discuss the right structure during consultation.

**Q: Cedar vs vinyl vs aluminum — which should I pick?**
A: Cedar for traditional warmth and the lowest entry cost; expect re-staining every 3–5 years. Vinyl for true zero-maintenance and white-trim aesthetic. Aluminum for modern lines and longest service life. Louvered for premium adjustable control.

**Q: Can the pergola integrate with lighting, fans, and heaters?**
A: Yes — we wire low-voltage and line-voltage lighting, ceiling fans (where overhead structure permits), and infrared heaters. All electrical is permitted and inspected.
