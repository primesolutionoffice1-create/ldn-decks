## H2 — "Patio Materials We Install"

### Concrete Pavers

Manufactured concrete pavers (Belgard, Techo-Bloc, Cambridge, EP Henry). Predictable shape, color range, and price. Best for straight-line modern patios and most residential budgets. Permeable options available.

### Natural Flagstone

Quarried stone (typically Pennsylvania bluestone, Tennessee crab orchard, or Arizona flagstone). Each piece is unique. Best for craftsman and traditional homes. Higher per-sq-ft cost, longer lifespan.

### Stamped Concrete

Single-pour concrete with stamped texture and integral color. Most cost-effective for large patios. Can be re-sealed every 3–5 years. Less natural-looking than flagstone, more uniform than pavers.

### Travertine

Natural stone with a softer, more uniform look than flagstone. Cooler underfoot in summer. Pool-deck favorite.

## H2 — "Permits, Drainage, and the NoVA Reality"

> Patios under 200 sq ft and not exceeding 30" of grade typically don't require a building permit in NoVA, but **drainage almost always matters**: NoVA's clay-heavy soil channels water back toward foundations if patios aren't graded correctly.
>
> When the patio integrates with electrical (outdoor kitchen, lighting), gas (fire feature), or significant grade change (retaining walls), a permit is required. We handle the permit when needed.
>
> Most HOAs require ARC approval for hardscape over a certain size or visible from the front of the home. We submit drawings as part of every project.

## H2 — "Patio FAQs"

**Q: How much does a patio cost in Northern Virginia?**
A: A 400 sq ft paver patio typically runs $7,000–$16,000 installed in NoVA, depending on paver tier and site prep. Stamped concrete is the most budget-friendly at $12–$20/sq ft. Flagstone (cut/fitted) is the premium tier at $40–$65/sq ft. Outdoor kitchens, lighting, and fire features add separately.

**Q: Pavers vs flagstone vs stamped concrete — which should I choose?**
A: Pavers for predictable color and price, with strong durability. Flagstone for natural beauty and timeless look — best for craftsman and traditional homes. Stamped concrete for the lowest cost on large patios. Full comparison: [paver vs flagstone](/paver-vs-flagstone-patio-northern-virginia).

**Q: Do I need a permit for a patio in NoVA?**
A: Most ground-level patios don't require a building permit. Patios with retaining walls over 4 ft, electrical integration (outdoor kitchen, lighting), or gas (fire features) typically do. We handle the permit when needed.

**Q: How long does a patio installation take?**
A: A typical 400 sq ft paver patio takes 5–10 build days depending on weather and site prep. Flagstone takes longer (more cutting). Outdoor kitchen integrations add 1–2 weeks for utility coordination.

**Q: Can you integrate a deck and patio in one project?**
A: Yes — many NoVA homes benefit from a hybrid: an elevated deck off the kitchen + a ground-level patio for the lower yard. We design and build both in a single project with consistent material flow. See [Deck vs Patio comparison](/deck-vs-patio-which-is-right).

**Q: What about drainage?**
A: Critical in NoVA's clay soil. We grade every patio to direct water away from the home and use permeable bases where appropriate. For large patios on flat lots, we install French drains or surface drains.

**Q: Can you add a fire pit or outdoor kitchen?**
A: Yes — gas or wood-burning fire features and full outdoor kitchens (grill, prep counter, sink, fridge) are common adds. All gas and electrical integrations are permitted and inspected.

**Q: How long does a patio last?**
A: Concrete pavers 25–50+ years (re-leveling every 10–15 years for settled areas). Flagstone 50+ years. Stamped concrete 20–30 years (re-sealing every 3–5 years). Travertine 50+ years.
