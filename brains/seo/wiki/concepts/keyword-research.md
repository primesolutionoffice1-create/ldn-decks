# Keyword Research

Keyword research has fundamentally changed. Before ChatGPT and recent Google algorithm updates, we used to spend 4-5 hours meticulously analyzing keywords. Today, with AI assistance and the growing importance of topical authority, our approach is more streamlined and strategic.

**🔑 Key Insight:** Content creation is now inexpensive, allowing us to target more keywords without extensive deliberation. This shifts our focus from "*which keywords should we target?*" to "*how do we build comprehensive topical authority?*"

While keyword research remains important, it's no longer the cornerstone of SEO strategy. Here's what has changed:

* **Topical relevance** now carries more weight in Google's algorithm  
* AI tools help us generate keyword variations quickly  
* Content creation costs have dropped dramatically  
* We can target broader keyword clusters rather than individual terms

Starting Point: Assessing Current Performance

When evaluating a website, your first step is to analyze their non-branded keyword traffic. Ask yourself:

* How much traffic comes from non-branded searches?  
* What's their current domain rating?  
* What competitive landscape are they facing?

**Note:** Branded keywords are easy to rank for—focus your efforts on non-branded opportunities that drive new visitors.

The One URL, One Keyword Rule

This is critical: **Each URL gets ONE target keyword.**

You cannot effectively optimize a single page for multiple keywords. While this might seem limiting, remember:

* One optimized keyword often ranks for multiple variations  
* Similar queries tend to produce similar search results  
* Your page will naturally capture related long-tail keywords

Example: Personal Injury Keywords

Consider "**personal injury lawyer**" vs. "**personal injury attorney**":

* Google sees these as nearly identical  
* Search results are almost the same  
* Ranking for one typically means ranking for both  
* Add their search volumes together when evaluating opportunity

**🧪 Quick Test:** Type both variations into Google. If the results are similar, you can count them as one keyword opportunity with combined search volume.

Managing Client Expectations

Clients often suggest keywords that are unrealistic for their current authority level. A common scenario:

* Client has a domain rating of 5  
* They want to rank for keywords requiring a domain rating of 80  
* They're not prepared to invest millions in link building

Your job is to find achievable targets that can build momentum and gradually increase their authority.

What We're Looking For

* **High volume** (relative to the client's niche)  
* **Low competition** (appropriate for their domain authority)  
* **Topical relevance** (builds subject matter expertise)

Context Matters

Volume is relative to your client's business:

* **High-value B2B:** 20-30 clicks/month might be excellent (if each client is worth $50,000+)  
* **E-commerce:** 10,000 clicks/month might be a rounding error

Always evaluate keyword opportunities within the context of your client's business model and revenue per visitor.

The Modern Approach: Quantity Over Perfection

Since AI has made content creation inexpensive, our strategy has evolved:

1. **Cast a wider net** \- Target all relevant keywords in your niche  
2. **Build topical clusters** \- Create comprehensive coverage of related topics  
3. **Let Google decide** \- Publish more content and see what performs  
4. **Iterate based on data** \- Improve what works, prune what doesn't

**Remember:** We used to agonize over keyword selection because content was expensive. Now, we can afford to be more experimental.

Calculating True Search Volume

When evaluating keywords:

1. **Identify similar queries** using Google's search results  
2. **Add search volumes** for keywords that will likely rank together  
3. **Calculate weighted average** of difficulty scores  
4. **Make decisions** based on combined opportunity

This gives you a more accurate picture of the traffic potential for each content piece.
