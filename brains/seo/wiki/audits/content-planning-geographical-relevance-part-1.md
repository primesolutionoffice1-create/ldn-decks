# Content Planning: Geographical Relevance Part 1

Welcome to Phase 3 of the Local SEO process\! After establishing strong topical relevance in Phase 2, it's time to focus on overcoming proximity challenges and expanding your reach across your service area.

![[images/image19.png]]

When to Start Building Geographical Relevance

Before diving into geographical content creation, you need to ensure your foundation is solid. Here's how to know you're ready:

* **Check your rank map**: Roughly one-third of your tracked locations should be ranking well (positions 1-3)  
* **Pattern recognition**: You'll notice rankings are strongest near your business location and drop off with distance  
* **Goal clarity**: Remember, proximity is a major ranking factor we're working to overcome

💡 **Quick Tip**: Don't rush into Phase 3\! Ensure your topical relevance (Phase 2\) is well-established first. Check out the lesson on [Building More Topical Relevance](https://www.skool.com/ai-seo-mastery-pro/classroom/63a81f62?md=a92c962b3fc24a76972646e306bb6b32) if you need a refresher.

The Strategic Approach to Geographic Content

Step 1: Identify Target Opportunities

Look for ranking positions that are *almost* there:

* **Primary targets**: Positions 4-5  
* **Secondary targets**: Position 6  
* **Why these positions?**: A 1-2 position improvement here means breaking into the coveted top 3\!

Step 2: Find Geographic Landmarks

Once you've identified a position 4-6 ranking location:

1. Zoom in on your rank tracking tool  
2. Cross-reference with Google Maps  
3. Identify nearby landmarks such as:  
   * Neighborhood names  
   * Parks or recreational areas  
   * Lakes or natural features  
   * Monuments or historical sites  
   * Major intersections  
   * Highway interchanges  
   * Shopping centers  
   * Schools or universities

📍 **Example**: If you find a position 5 ranking near a major highway interchange, that interchange becomes your geographic landmark for content creation.

Step 3: Create Targeted Geographic Content

Your content formula is simple but powerful:

**\[Service/Category\]** \+ near \+ **\[Geographic Landmark\]** \+ in \+ **\[City\]**

For example:

* "Water Heater Replacement near the I-10 and 610 Interchange in Houston"  
* "Emergency Plumbing near Memorial Park in Houston"  
* "Drain Cleaning near Rice University in Houston"

Content Structure for Geographic Pages

Focus your content on one of these angles...

1. **Project Experience**  
   * Describe specific jobs completed near the landmark  
   * Include challenges unique to that area  
   * Mention repeat customers or referrals from the neighborhood  
2. **Area-Specific Considerations**  
   * What makes servicing this area different?  
   * Traffic patterns or access challenges  
   * Local regulations or requirements  
   * Typical home styles or plumbing systems in the area  
3. **Landmark Significance**  
   * Interesting facts about the landmark  
   * How it affects your service delivery  
   * Why residents near this landmark choose your services

🎯 The I-10 and 610 interchange example is perfect because it's one of the widest interstates in the US. You could discuss traffic challenges, timing considerations, and how your team navigates these obstacles to provide timely service.

Creating Your Locations Hub Page

Every local SEO strategy needs a central locations page that:

Structure:

1. **Navigation placement**: Link from your main navigation with simple "Locations" text  
2. **Overview content**: Write 200-300 words about your overall service area  
3. **Hub functionality**: Link to all individual geographic content pages  
4. **Bidirectional linking**: Each geographic page should link back to the main locations page

Content for the Hub Page:

* General discussion of your service across the entire city  
* What makes your city unique from your service perspective  
* Your commitment to serving all neighborhoods  
* Years of experience in the area

⏩⏩ **Related Lesson**: If you haven't created your locations page as part of the Core 30, review [[the-core-30-content-strategy|How to Structure the Core 30]] for guidance.

![[images/image20.png]]  
Essential Elements for Each Geographic Page

1\. Driving Directions

Always include turn-by-turn directions from the landmark to your Google Business Profile location. This:

* Reinforces the geographic connection  
* Provides genuine utility to visitors  
* Strengthens local relevance signals

2\. External Link Requirement

Every geographic relevance page needs at least one external backlink to be effective. This could come from:

* Local directories  
* Neighborhood associations  
* Community blogs  
* Local news sites

⚠️ **Important**: Don't skip the external linking step\! Review [**Introduction to Backlinking**](https://www.skool.com/ai-seo-mastery-pro/classroom/63a81f62?md=13ed811bf5d844d99912d8bc2c9b7988) and [Searching for Link Opportunities](https://www.skool.com/ai-seo-mastery-pro/classroom/63a81f62?md=bebd9d05586e4daebdb0b82996c1e51e) for strategies.

Scaling Your Geographic Strategy

Example: Chicago Neighborhoods

Chicago provides an excellent model with its distinct neighborhood structure:

* Created dozens of neighborhood-specific pages  
* Each linked from a central locations page  
* Targeted local search terms for each area

Adapting to Your Market

Not every city has Chicago's neighborhood structure, but every area has landmarks:

* **Suburban areas**: Shopping centers, schools, major intersections  
* **Rural areas**: County landmarks, highways, natural features  
* **Dense urban areas**: Districts, subway stops, cultural landmarks

Measuring Success

Track your progress by monitoring:

* Movement of positions 4-6 into the top 3  
* Overall map coverage improvement  
* Expansion of your "green zone" on rank tracking maps  
* Increase in calls/leads from previously weak areas
