## Context:

The imported Local SEO Knowledge Base teaches a local SEO system built around Google Business Profile visibility, Core 30 site structure, topical relevance, geographic relevance, schema, backlinks, citations, GBP completeness, and AI/LLM visibility.

For LDN Decks, the system must be applied to high-value deck building outcomes in Northern Virginia: qualified phone calls, composite deck leads, deck replacement leads, Trex/TimberTech/AZEK authority, Google Maps visibility, local organic visibility, AI search mentions, and trusted local entity signals.

The operating rule is simple: every SEO asset should either improve GBP trust, improve topical relevance, improve geographic relevance, improve conversion quality, or strengthen the business entity across trusted sources.

## Findings:

Local SEO is GBP-first. The map pack captures most local-search action, so the website is not only a website; it is the relevance and trust engine that helps the GBP rank.

The strongest structure is a Core 30 foundation: one page per GBP category, one page per important service, strong About and Contact pages, and a homepage/GBP landing page aligned with the primary category plus city or region.

For LDN Decks, the Core 30 should map to deck-builder revenue, not generic traffic. Priority service clusters are composite decks, Trex decking, TimberTech decking, AZEK/PVC decking, deck replacement, covered decks, deck repair, railings, lighting, outdoor kitchens, and luxury outdoor living.

Topical relevance should come before geographic expansion. Google and AI systems first need to understand that LDN Decks is deeply authoritative on deck construction, composite materials, deck replacement, structural repair, outdoor living, railings, and Northern Virginia building considerations.

Geographic relevance should be layered after the service foundation, especially around Ashburn, Leesburg, Sterling, Fairfax, McLean, Reston, Arlington, Vienna, Great Falls, Loudoun County, Fairfax County, and Northern Virginia landmarks/neighborhoods.

GBP changes can be live-impact and risky. Services, categories, business information, address, hours, automated calls, posts, photos, and review responses must be drafted and approved before publishing.

Schema is a fast win. LDN Decks should use Organization schema broadly and HomeAndConstructionBusiness/LocalBusiness schema on the GBP landing page, plus Service, FAQ, Review, AggregateRating, Breadcrumb, and Article schema where appropriate.

Backlinks split into two jobs: validation links for new/changed content and trust links from real local or industry sources. For LDN Decks, the highest-value trust links are NADRA, Trex/TimberTech/AZEK directories, local chambers, Loudoun/Fairfax community organizations, sponsorships, local media, BBB, Apple Maps, Bing Places, Yelp, Houzz, BuildZoom, and contractor directories.

AI search visibility requires consistent entity signals across trusted places, schema that uses natural homeowner language, direct-answer FAQs, real reviews/testimonials, and non-promotional community mentions.

## Risks:

Changing GBP categories, services, business info, address, hours, or automated-call settings can trigger ranking volatility or profile review/suspension.

Publishing low-quality duplicate city pages can dilute trust and create doorway-page risk.

Adding services that LDN Decks does not actually provide can create bad leads and GBP trust issues.

Aggressive external anchor text can look unnatural. External links should use mostly branded, generic, partial-match, and natural variation anchors.

Hidden website content should not carry important SEO information. Important service and FAQ content should be visible by default.

AI/Reddit/community visibility must never sound promotional or fake. Drafts should be realistic, human, and non-spammy.
